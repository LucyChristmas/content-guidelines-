# Page introductions and meta descriptions

Both fields are required for all pages in the CMS. They serve different purposes and appear in different places.

---

## Quick reference

| Field | Purpose | Where it appears | Character limit | Audience |
|-------|---------|------------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Meta description** | Search engine snippet | Google search results (not on your page) | 105 characters for mobile, 155 for desktop | People searching Google |
| **Page introduction** | On-page content | Top of your page, below the heading | 1-2 sentences (aim for 150-200 characters) | People on your page |

---

## Meta description

### What is a meta description?

A meta description is a brief summary that appears in search engine results. It describes your page content and encourages people to click through to your page.

**Where it appears:** Google search results (below your page title)

**Where it doesn't appear:** On your actual web page

### Purpose

Meta descriptions help:
- People decide if your page answers their question
- Search engines understand your page content
- Improve click-through rates from search results

### How to write a good meta description

**Do:**
- Write 105 characters for mobile (this is most important)
- Can extend to 155 characters for desktop if needed
- Include your main keyword naturally
- Make it compelling and accurate
- Use active voice
- Focus on what the user will find or learn
- Start with the most important information

**Don't:**
- Duplicate your page heading
- Stuff with keywords
- Use generic descriptions like "Learn more" or "Click here"
- Include quotation marks (they get cut off in results)
- Start with "The University of Otago" (wastes valuable characters and Google often adds this automatically)

### Examples

**Poor meta description:**
"Information about scholarships at Otago. Click here to learn more about our scholarship opportunities."

**Good meta description:**
"Explore scholarships for undergraduate and postgraduate study at Otago. Find funding options, eligibility criteria, and application deadlines."

**Real examples from our website:**

*Health Sciences First Year:*
"Health Sciences First Year is a common first year for dentistry, medical laboratory science, medicine, pharmacy and physiotherapy."

*Staff intranet:*
"Log in to access staff resources, policies, forms, IT support, and workplace information for University of Otago employees."

*Pacific at Otago:*
"Support and community for Pacific students at Otago, including scholarships, mentoring, cultural events, and academic help."

## Page introduction

### What is a page introduction?

A page introduction is the opening text that appears at the top of your page, directly below the main heading. It sets context and helps readers understand what the page covers.

**Where it appears:** On your page, visible to all visitors

**Where it doesn't appear:** In search results

### Purpose

Page introductions help:
- Orient readers when they land on your page
- Provide context for the content below
- Improve readability and user experience
- Keep readers on your page

### How to write a good page introduction

**Do:**
- Write 1-2 sentences (aim for 150-200 characters)
- Front-load the most important information
- Use plain language
- Make it relevant to the page heading
- Address what readers will find on the page

**Don't:**
- Repeat the page heading word-for-word
- Write more than 2 sentences
- Use jargon or complex terminology
- Make it generic or vague

### Examples

**Poor page introduction:**
"Welcome to our scholarships page where you can find information about scholarships."

**Good page introduction:**
"We offer a range of scholarships for domestic and international students. Use this page to search available scholarships, check eligibility, and learn how to apply."

**Real examples from our website:**

*Health Sciences First Year:*
"HSFY is your pathway to dentistry, medical laboratory science, medicine, pharmacy and physiotherapy. It's also a great start for degrees in biomedical and biological sciences."

*Staff intranet:*
"Find everything you need as a University of Otago staff member. Access HR forms, IT help, policies, news, and tools to support your work."

*Pacific at Otago:*
"We're here to support your success at Otago. Connect with our Pacific community, find scholarships and mentoring, and access academic and pastoral support."

## Key differences

### Meta description = for search engines
- Appears in Google results
- Attracts people to click
- Optimised for searchers
- 105 characters for mobile (can extend to 155 for desktop)

### Page introduction = for visitors
- Appears on your page
- Orients people who've arrived
- Optimised for readers
- Aim for 150-200 characters (1-2 sentences)

---

## Writing both fields

Since both are required, think of them as serving different stages of the user journey:

1. **Meta description** convinces someone to click from Google
2. **Page introduction** confirms they're in the right place once they arrive

They should complement each other but not duplicate each other.

### Example for the same page

**Page heading:** Health Sciences First Year

**Meta description (for Google):**
"Health Sciences First Year is a common first year for dentistry, medical laboratory science, medicine, pharmacy and physiotherapy."

**Page introduction (on the page):**
"HSFY is your pathway to dentistry, medical laboratory science, medicine, pharmacy and physiotherapy. It's also a great start for degrees in biomedical and biological sciences."

Notice how they cover similar ground but serve different purposes:
- Meta description is **concise and factual** - gives the essential information for searchers
- Page introduction is **slightly expanded** - adds context about other degree options once users arrive

## Mobile considerations

**Meta descriptions on mobile devices:**
- Google displays approximately 105 characters on mobile
- This is significantly less than desktop (155 characters)
- **Always prioritise mobile** - write for 105 characters first
- Most important information must appear in first 105 characters
- If you have additional context that's useful for desktop, you can extend to 155 characters
- Most users search on mobile, so mobile display is more critical

**Page introductions on mobile:**
- Keep to 1-2 sentences maximum
- Mobile screens show less text at once
- Shorter introductions are easier to read on small screens

## Common mistakes to avoid

### Mistake 1: Making them identical
❌ **Don't:** Copy and paste the same text into both fields

✅ **Do:** Write unique content for each field based on its purpose

### Mistake 2: Being too generic
❌ **Don't:** "Learn more about our programmes and what we offer"

✅ **Do:** "Explore our five undergraduate degrees in health sciences, from dentistry to pharmacy"

### Mistake 3: Exceeding character limits
❌ **Don't:** Write long paragraphs in either field

✅ **Do:** Keep meta descriptions under 105 characters (mobile priority), page introductions to 1-2 sentences

### Mistake 4: Starting with the organisation name
❌ **Don't:** "The University of Otago offers scholarships for undergraduate students..."

✅ **Do:** "Explore scholarships for undergraduate students. Find funding options, eligibility, and deadlines."

**Why:** "The University of Otago" uses 24+ characters. Google often adds your organisation name automatically, and users already see "otago.ac.nz" in the URL. Use those characters for compelling content instead.

### Mistake 5: Forgetting keywords
❌ **Don't:** Miss the opportunity to include relevant search terms

✅ **Do:** Include terms people actually search for (naturally, not stuffed)
