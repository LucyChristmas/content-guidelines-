# University of Otago Writing Style & Terminology Guide
## Reorganized for Clarity and AI Usage

---

## 1. FOUNDATIONAL SPELLING & GRAMMAR STANDARDS

### Primary Language Standard
**British English spelling** is mandatory (not American English)
- colour (not color)
- analyse (not analyze)
- neighbour (not neighbor)
- organisation (not organization)
- favourite (not favorite)
- traveller (not traveler)
- programme (not program - except computer programs)

**Reference:** New Zealand Oxford Dictionary is the prime authority

### Use 's' not 'z'
Use 's' instead of 'z' for words which could be spelled either way, except when part of an official name (e.g., World Health Organization)
- categorise
- organise
- organisation
- randomisation

### Use '-er' not '-or'
The -er ending is preferred over -or, with one exception:
- adviser (not advisor)
- convener (not convenor)
- **Exception:** supervisor (not superviser)

## 2. NUMBERS & MEASUREMENTS

### Numbers - General Rules
**Below 10:** Spell out (one–nine)
**10 and above:** Use numerals (10–999,999)
- Use commas from 1,000 to avoid confusion with years
**1 million and above:** Numbers and words (1 million, 2.5 billion)

**Always spell out** numbers when used as the first word of a sentence:
- ✅ Fifteen students attended
- ❌ 15 students attended

**Exception:** If a sentence contains many numbers (some over 10, some below), use numerals throughout for easier reading
- ✅ There were 2 pencils, 10 pens, 36 markers and 16 notebooks

**Web writing:** Numerals are preferred as they are easier to read on screen

### Weights and Measures
**No space** between number and measure, except computer memory

**Area:**
- hectare (ha)
- square centimetre (cm²), square metre (m²)
- cubic centimetre (cm³), cubic metre (m³)
- Example: Over 31,000ha around Dunedin

**Computer Memory (with space):**
- kilobyte (KB), megabyte (MB), gigabyte (GB), terabyte (TB)
- Example: The new computer had just 40 GB of memory

**Length/Distance/Speed:**
- millimetre (mm), centimetre (cm), metre (m), kilometre (km)
- kilometres per hour (kph)
- Example: The average hand span is about 20cm
- Example: The driver averaged 80kph

**Power:**
- watt (W), kilowatt (kW), megawatts (MW), gigawatts (GW)
- megawatt/hours (MWh), gigawatt/hours (GWh)

**Temperature:**
- Celsius (C), Fahrenheit (F)
- **Write out in full** rather than using abbreviations
- Example: The predicted rise was two or three degrees Celsius

**Volume/Capacity:**
- millilitre (ml), litre (l)

**Weight/Mass:**
- gram (g), kilogram (kg), pound (lb), ounce (oz), tonne (t)

### Ordinals
Spell out **first through ninth**, then **10th, 11th**, etc.
**Exception:** Dates do not use ordinals

### Ages
- The child was just two years old
- A two-year-old should not be expected to sit still
- Enrolments were open for three- and four-year-olds

### Percentages
**Use '%' only in:**
- Financial documents
- Academic marks/scores

**All other instances:** Spell out "per cent" (two words) or "percentage"

### Dollars
- NZ$1,234
- US$1,234
- NZ$15 million

## 3. DATES & TIME

### Date Format
**Standard:** date month year (no ordinals)
**Include day of week whenever possible**

**Acceptable formats:**
- Wednesday, 27 January 2016
- 24 Feb 2017
- ❌ 11th November 1997
- ❌ November 11, 1997
- ❌ The 11th of November

**Numbers 1–9:** Written as single digit
- ✅ 2 January 2001
- ❌ 02 January 2001

**Month abbreviations:**
Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec

**Date ranges:** Use dash
- 16–18 July 2017
- Winter lasts from June to August (use "to" when using "from")
- ❌ The programme runs from Oct–Dec

**Web writing:** Use full date including year for context and trustworthiness

### Time
**Use 12-hour clock** with colon between hour and minutes
**am/pm** with no full stops

**Acceptable formats:**
- 10:55am, 3:45pm
- 12:00–1:30pm, 3:30 to 4:00pm
- 2–4pm, 11am to 7pm
- 5am (NZT)
- ❌ 11.52 p.m.

**Abbreviations for units of time:**
- second (sec), minutes (min), hours (hr)

### Decades
**No apostrophe** with 's' when listed in full:
- ✅ 1990s, 1840s
- ❌ 1960's, 20's

**Contracted decades:** Use correct apostrophe direction
- ✅ '80s (contraction of 1980s)

### Seasons
**Not capitalised**
Use **autumn** (not fall)

### Events and Holidays
**Use upper case** for public holidays and major events:
- Christmas Day, Christmas Eve
- New Year's Day, New Year's Eve

## 4. CAPITALIZATION RULES

### General Principles
**Capitalize:**
- Proper nouns
- Official titles when used with a name
- Specific departments, programs, degrees when referring to the specific entity
- First word after a colon in titles/headings

**Lower case:**
- Job titles used generally (not as someone's title)
- Office holders referred to by office alone
- Generic references to subjects, degrees, or departments

### Job Titles
**Capitalize only when used as someone's title:**
- ✅ Professor Jane Redmond would be the night's big winner
- ✅ The professors realized the students were nervous
- ✅ The Director of Resources took his place
- ❌ The Directors were first to be notified

### People and Titles
**Upper case** for ranks and titles when written with a name
**Lower case** when on their own
- ✅ Queen Elizabeth was set to attend
- ✅ The queen resides in Buckingham Palace
- ✅ The prime minister met his wife at Otago
- ✅ In this country, the prime minister is elected...

### Departments and Subjects
**Use capitals for:**
- Department name: Department of History
- Subject name when referring to University study: He majored in Microbiology
- Paper name: Taking Earth and Ocean Science
- Programme name: Distance Learning, Health Sciences First Year
- Context: Students can enter Dentistry in their second year

**Use lower case:**
- Generic references: If you have a mathematics background from school
- General careers: Are you interested in a career in social work?

### Organizations
**Capitalize** when full name is used:
- Ministry of Education
- Department of Pathology
- Treaty of Waitangi

**Lower case** when referring generally:
- The new health act was finally released

### Places and Geography
**Use upper case** for definite geographical places:
- New Zealand
- the South Island
- the South Pacific
- Northern Hemisphere, Southern Hemisphere
- the Pacific Northwest

**Use Māori name first** when mentioning NZ cities in a story:
- Ōtepoti Dunedin
- Ōtautahi Christchurch
- Choose one for subsequent mentions

### Specific Terms
**Indigenous** - capital I always
**Black** - capital B when referring to groups in racial, ethnic or cultural terms
**Covid-19** - Upper case C, lower case after, hyphenated to 19
**coalition Government** - lower case c, upper case G
**Internet** - Capitalize
**web** - Lowercase unless starting sentence
**Year 13** - Capital Y and numeral for school years (not year nine)
**Deaf/deaf** - Capitalize when referring to the Deaf community, lowercase when referring to being deaf in general
**Earth/earth** - Capitalize when referring to the planet, lowercase for ground/soil

## 5. PUNCTUATION

### Full Stop (Period)
- Use at end of sentences
- Use after last item in bulleted list that forms complete sentence
- Insert only **one space** after a full stop

**Do NOT use full stops:**
- In most abbreviations/acronyms (exceptions: e.g., i.e., c.)
- In initials
- At end of sentences ending with web or email address
- In headings

### Comma
Use commas to aid understanding and break up long text. Don't over-saturate.

**Oxford (Serial) Comma:**
Use **only when necessary** to improve clarity and avoid confusion
- ✅ WITH: Highlights included the President, an ancient yoga expert, and a pen collector
- ✅ WITHOUT: Highlights included the President, an ancient yoga expert and a pen collector
- (Use WITH if the President being an expert would cause confusion)

**Web writing:** Commas affect screen reader vocalization for accessibility

### Colon :
**Use to:**
- Introduce a list
- Separate title from subtitle

**Format:**
- Use lower case after colon when introducing a list
- Use capital letter after colon within title/heading/headline
- **No colon** needed if list follows a heading directly

Examples:
- Your email must include: name, job title, department
- Bodies and bugs: Curious kids doing cool science
- ❌ FORMER PRESIDENTS: (no colon needed)

### Semicolon ;
**Use to:**
- Separate two independent clauses (each could be standalone sentences)
- Replace commas in complex lists for clarity

Examples:
- The lecturer wore thick glasses; he often misplaced them
- I visited Toronto, Ontario; Regina, Saskatchewan; Calgary, Alberta
- ❌ Dunedin is famous for having; the steepest street (incorrect usage)

### Apostrophe '
**Use for:**
- Possession: the lecturer's office (singular), the lecturers' timetables (plural)
- Contractions: it's (it is), don't (do not)

**Never use** to form plural nouns
- ❌ The basket's were filled with apples

**Note:** Hawke's Bay and Arthur's Pass are only two NZ place names with possessive apostrophes

### Quotation Marks " "
**Use double quotes** unless quote within a quote (then single)
- "It's 'just like a bought one', as my brother would say"

**Single letter:** Use single quotes
- The sentence should have started with 'M'

**Question/exclamation marks:**
No full stop needed after closing quotation marks
- ✅ "It's a fire!"
- ❌ "What did you want to talk about?".

**Fragment quotes:** Closing quotation mark inside full stop
- She said she was "very pleased with the result".

**Multiple paragraphs:** Opening quotes on each paragraph, closing quotes only on final paragraph

### Dashes and Hyphens
**Dash (–):** Separate information or provide break, **with spaces**
- The students – like so many others – were delayed

**Dash for ranges:** Connect related values, **no spaces**
- World War II lasted from 1939–1945
- Pages 10–30
- The score was 21–14

**Hyphen (-):** Join words, separate syllables, in certain adjectives, double-barrelled names
- free-range eggs, on-campus, Joseph Gordon-Levitt

### Parentheses ( )
Use to suggest related yet unnecessary information

**Punctuation placement:**
- Inside closing parenthesis: if complete sentence is in parentheses
- Outside closing parenthesis: otherwise

Examples:
- The lecture will start at 4:30pm (which is later than usual).
- The lecture will start at 4:30pm. (That's later than usual.)

### Ellipses ...
Use three dots to indicate intentional omission. **Space either side.**
Don't overuse for trailing thoughts or pauses.

**Web writing:** Omit spaces to avoid unintentional line breaks

### Slash /
Use as substitute for "or" with **no space** on either side
- male/female, Y/N, n/a
- **Exception:** Use spaces if separated items include spaces

**Web:** Spaces can be omitted/inserted to avoid misleading line breaks

### Ampersand &
**Use only** when part of a proper name (e.g., Fisher & Paykel)
**Spell out** "and" in all other instances
**Never** in Otago department formal names

**Web exception:** Permissible in navigation to save space when critically necessary

## 6. COMMONLY CONFUSED WORDS

### its / it's
- **its** = possessive pronoun (The cat lost its tail)
- **it's** = contraction of "it is" (It's another sunny day)

### their / there / they're
- **they're** = contraction of "they are" (They're having lunch)
- **their** = possessive pronoun (Their desk is messy)
- **there** = place or existence (Over there / There is an event)

### effect / affect
**As verbs:**
- **effect** = bring about (The manager effected changes)
- **affect** = have impact on (Weather will affect the number of people)

**As nouns:**
- **effect** = the impact something causes (What effect did it have?)
- **affect** = outward appearance of psychological state (Took news with little affect)

### that / which
- **that** = meaning affected if information is removed (The pants that I brought)
- **which** = sentence still makes sense without it (The book, which also lacks a map)

### between / among
- **between** = separating two things
- **among** = separating more than two (not amongst)

### less / fewer
- **fewer** = things you can count (fewer freckles)
- **less** = things you can't count (less confidence)

### e.g. / i.e.
- **e.g.** = for example
- **i.e.** = that is, in other words

### a / an
**Use "an" before:**
- Silent 'h': an heir, an honour, an hour
- Abbreviations that sound like vowels: an EdD, an LLB, an MB ChB

**Use "a" before:**
- Aspirated 'h': a hotel, a historian
- Abbreviations that sound like consonants: a BSc, a PhD

## 10. ABBREVIATIONS & ACRONYMS

### General Rules
- Spell out full name with acronym in brackets on first mention
- Use acronym only in subsequent references
- No need to spell out when abbreviation is more familiar than full form (BBC, FBI, DNA)
- Capitalize each letter, no punctuation
- Plurals: Use small 's' with no apostrophe (FAQs, PhDs)
- Possessives: Follow usual rules (The BMS's Department)

### Standard Abbreviations to Use
- **e.g.** (for example) - with full stops
- **i.e.** (that is) - with full stops
- **etc.** - with full stop
- **CV** - no full stops
- **Dr** - no full stop
- **c.** (circa, means "about") - italicized, full stop, no space before numbers but space before words

### Initials
**No full stops, space between multiple initials:**
- Associate Professor Charles M Burns
- Professor P D Barnes
- **Exception:** Match official names (W.H. Oliver Prize)

## 11. FORMATTING & EMPHASIS

### Bold
Use **sparingly** to emphasize important text (dates, names, deadlines)

**Punctuation following bold text should NOT be bold** unless entire sentence is bold
- ✅ The meeting would take place on **Wednesday 6 July**.
- ✅ The award was presented to **Dr Alan Skinner** for his efforts.
- ❌ The final day is **Friday.**

### Italics
**Use for:**
- Names of boats, ships, books, movies, newspapers, musical works, plays, films, journals, publications
- Latin phrases: in utero, in vitro, in vivo
- Paper codes and titles in large text blocks: CHEM 111 Molecular Architecture

**Avoid using quotation marks** for these items

**Web exception:** If CMS doesn't permit italics, quotation marks acceptable (but never in event listing titles)

### Underlining
**Avoid** - suggests hyperlinked content
Use bold or italics instead for emphasis

### Bullet Points / Lists
**General:** If no particular order, alphabetize
**Punctuation preceding list:** Colon (unless list follows heading directly)

**List of items (no particular order):**
- Begin each point lower case (unless proper noun)
- No punctuation at end

**Points forming complete sentence with preceding text:**
- Begin each point lower case
- Full stop only at end of final item

**Points that are full sentences:**
- Begin each with upper case
- Full stop at end of each

**Web writing:**
- Start each point with capital letter
- No punctuation at end of any points
- No colon at end of heading if list immediately follows

## 12. WEB & EMAIL GUIDELINES

### Web Addresses
**Generally omit** http:// and www (check if critically necessary)
**Include https:** for secure sites if needed
**No full stop** at end of sentence with web address
**No underlining** in print
**Generally don't capitalize** within address (unless functions as brand name like Outlook.com)

Examples:
- ✅ otago.ac.nz
- ✅ otago.ac.nz/healthsciences
- ❌ www.otago.ac.nz
- ❌ Otago.ac.nz
- ❌ otago.ac.nz/HealthSciences

### Contact Details
**Order:** (use only relevant details, no punctuation, one space after subheading)
- Freephone 0800 889 900 (within New Zealand)
- Tel +64 3 479 1100
- Mob +64 27 479 1100
- Fax +64 3 479 5417
- Email joe.bloggs@otago.ac.nz
- Web otago.ac.nz

**Phone numbers - acceptable formats:**
- 479 1100
- 479 1100 ext 8567
- 03 479 1100
- 021 279 110
- 64 3 479 1100
- 64 21 279 1100

**Preferred for web:** International format (+64 3 479 1100)
**Include prefixes** for mobile users

### Email Addresses
**Capitalize 'E' only when:**
- First word in sentence
- Used for contact details

**Keep on one line** wherever possible
**Use address itself** as link text (no capital letters within address)

Examples:
- ✅ Email joe.bloggs@otago.ac.nz
- ❌ Email Joe.Bloggs@otago.ac.nz
- ❌ Email Joe Bloggs
- ❌ Email us

### Headings/Headlines
**Web specific:**
- Avoid one-word headings/sub-headings
- Use heading levels in correct order (H1 → H2 → H3 → H4)
- H1 is page name in large font
- Must use H2 before H3, H3 before H4, etc.

## 14. ACADEMIC CONVENTIONS

### Degrees and Diplomas
Always name and abbreviate correctly with capitals/lower case as defined

**bachelor's / master's degrees:**
- Lower case 'b' or 'm' unless referring to specific degree (Bachelor of Arts)
- Apostrophe position differentiates singular/plural:
  - a bachelor's degree
  - a degree at master's level
  - all bachelors' degrees will be awarded
  - holders of masters' degrees

**Doctor of Philosophy (PhD):**
- In most instances, use abbreviation only

**double degree:**
- Standard term for two degrees simultaneously (e.g., BA BCom)

**honours degree:**
- Lower case 'h' in general, capitals when part of degree name
- No apostrophe
- BSc(Hons)
- Bachelor of Science with Honours

**honorary doctorate:**
- Lower case in general
- Capitals for specific degree: Honorary Doctor of Commerce

### Major / Minor
- **major** = compulsory papers to specialise in a subject (lower case 'm')
- **minor** = secondary specialization (lower case 'm')
- **double major** = single degree with two majors

### Papers
**Standard format:** Space between letters and numbers, italicize for clarity in large text blocks
- ✅ CHEM 111 Molecular Architecture
- ✅ CHEM 111
- ❌ CHEM111

### Level References
**Use lower case and hyphen:**
- a 100-level course
- doing a paper at 100-level

**Exception:** NCEA Level 3 (capital L and numeral)
- ❌ Dr Bloggs needed a speaker for his 200 level course

### Semester References
**No longer use:** "first semester" or "second semester"
**Use:** semester 1, semester 2
**Lower case 's'** except at sentence start

### Latin Suffixes
- alumnus/alumni (male graduate / plural men or mixed)
- alumna/alumnae (female graduate / plural women)
- emeritus/emeriti (retired male professor / plural)
- emerita/emeritae (retired female professor / plural)
- symposium/symposia (conference / plural)

### Alumni
Correct term for graduates or those who completed papers but didn't graduate/aren't currently enrolled. Can include ex-staff.
**Avoid "alum"** (chemical compound)

- alumni = plural men (or men and women)
- alumnus = single man
- alumna = single woman
- alumnae = plural women

## 15. TITLES & HONORIFICS

### Honorifics (Titles Before Names)
**No full stops:** Dr, Mr, Mrs, Miss, Ms

**First mention:** Full name with honorific
**Subsequent mentions:**
- Casual: First name only
- Formal/news: Family name with honorific

Examples:
- Dr Joseph Bloggs assembled the paperwork … Joseph was known for being meticulous
- Dr Joseph Bloggs assembled the paperwork … Dr Bloggs was known for being meticulous

**Where no title:** Use Mr, Mrs, Ms, or Miss after first mention

### Postnominals (After Names)
**Order:**
1. Official honours and appointments (ONZM JP)
2. Degrees in ascending order (BA MSc(Otago) PhD(Auck))
3. Diplomas from universities/tertiary institutions
4. Certificates from universities/tertiary institutions
5. Fellowships of learned/professional bodies
6. Memberships of learned/professional bodies

### Split Infinitives
**Admissible** when it would be ugly not to split
- ✅ to further inform (perfectly acceptable)

## 16. PRONOUNS & INCLUSIVE LANGUAGE

### Pronouns
**Singular:** he or she accordingly
**Plural:** they/their
**Neutral singular:** they/their acceptable when individual doesn't identify as male/female and requests neutral pronoun

Examples:
- Singular: He sat the exam alongside 135 students
- Plural: Derek and Sandra were departing. They hadn't packed yet.
- Neutral: "Language is always changing," they say

### Greetings (English & Te Reo Māori)
**Very formal:**
- Dear Sir/Madam = Kei te rangatira, tēnā koe

**Formal with name:**
- Dear Vice-Chancellor = E te Tumuaki, tēnā koe
- Dear Sir/Madam = Tēnā koe
- Dear Gerald = Tēnā koe, Gerald

**General:**
- Dear everybody = Tēnā koutou
- Hello/Hi = Kia ora
- Hello/Hi, Mark = Kia ora, Mark
- Hello/Hi everybody = Kia ora koutou

**Sign-offs:**
- That is all for now = Heoi anō tāku mō naianei
- Yours faithfully = Nāku, nā...
- Yours sincerely = Nāku noa, nā... / Nāhaku noa, nā...

**Multiple signers:**
- Two people: Nā [name 1] māua ko [name 2]
- Three+ people: Nā [name 1], mātou ko [name 2], ko [name 3]...

## 18. OTAGO-SPECIFIC TERMINOLOGY
*These items are specific to University of Otago*

### Official University Name & Translation
**Legal name:** University of Otago
**With article:** the University of Otago (if required for sense)
**Subsequent mentions:** the University or Otago

**Māori name:** Ōtākou Whakaihu Waka
- Metaphor meaning "a place of many firsts"
- Less literal than previous name Te Whare Wānanga o Otāgo
- Refers to education, research, student experiences, and being first NZ university

**Writing both names:**
- University of Otago – Ōtākou Whakaihu Waka, OR
- Ōtākou Whakaihu Waka – University of Otago
- Context determines order

**Social media:** English name first to build association with tohu
**Longer writing:** Follow language approach of document

**Campus references:**
- Don't add modifiers like 'ki Ōtautahi' to Māori name
- Use: University of Otago, Wellington – Ōtākou Whakaihu Waka, Pōneke

### Campus Locations
**Official names:**
- University of Otago Auckland Centre
- University of Otago, Christchurch
- University of Otago, Wellington
- University of Otago Wellington City Office
- University of Otago Southland Campus
- University of Otago College of Education

**Abbreviations:**
- UO for University of Otago (though Otago is preferred)
- UOC for University of Otago, Christchurch
- UOW for University of Otago, Wellington

**Campus convention:**
- Dunedin campus (not "the Dunedin campus")
