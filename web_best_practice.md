## FOUNDATIONAL WEB WRITING PRINCIPLES

### Key Difference: Web vs Print
**Web pages should be ~50% the length of similar print documents**

**Critical statistic:** Users only read about 18% of content on web pages

**Core requirements - content must be:**
- **T**o the point
- **R**elevant
- **A**ccessible
- **C**lear
- **E**ngaging
- **N**onthreatening
- **A**ble to be understood by various audiences

**(Mnemonic: TRACENA)**

### User Behavior on Web
**Users scan and skim, they don't read thoroughly**

**F-pattern scanning:** Users focus on:
- Top left side of page
- Headings
- First few words of sentences/lists
- **Average: only first 2 words per line**

**Content clarity test:** Your content is only clear if users can:
1. Find what they need
2. Understand what they find
3. Use what they find to meet their needs

## 1. CONTENT STRUCTURE & ORGANIZATION

### The Inverted Pyramid Style
**Most important information first**
- Begin with shortest, clearest statement about your topic
- Put critical information at top
- Background information at bottom

### Chunking Content
**One topic per paragraph** (rule of thumb)
- Split topics into logical sections
- Separate with informative headings
- Don't pack everything into long paragraphs

### Lists Over Paragraphs
**Prefer lists** - easier to scan

**Numbered lists:** Only use when sequence or count matters
**Bulleted lists:** For non-sequential items

### Only Necessary Information
Include only what users need for their top tasks
Omit unnecessary information

---

## 2. WRITING STYLE FOR WEB

### Sentence Length
**Target: 15-20 words per sentence** (Oxford Guide to Plain English)
- Short sentences = easier understanding
- Long sentences slow comprehension

### Active Voice (Preferred)
**Why:** Simpler, more direct, requires fewer words

**Examples:**
- ✅ Active: The student completed the form
- ❌ Passive: The form was completed by the student

- ✅ DO: Watching water boil won't make it boil any faster
- ❌ DON'T: It is a simple fact that continuing to keep regular checks and constantly watching cold water rising in temperature until it arrives at the boiling point of degree centigrade, will not, in fact, make it come to the perfect temperature any faster, than, say, staring at the nearest wallpaper

### Omit Unnecessary Words
**Lean phrases:**
- Use "to" (not "in order to")
- Use "examine" (not "carry out an examination of")
- Use "ask" (not "make an enquiry")

**Use strong verbs** instead of noun forms that hide the verb

### Familiar Language
The more familiar your words, the faster readers understand them

## 3. PLAIN LANGUAGE & READABILITY

### Plain Language Act 2022
**Required for:** All public service agencies and crown agents
**Recommended for:** Everyone else (highly recommended)

**Why it matters:**
Only 16% of adult New Zealanders have high literacy levels

**Resources:**
- digital.govt.nz/standards-and-guidance/design-and-ux/content-design-guidance/plain-language/
- digital.govt.nz/standards-and-guidance/design-and-ux/content-design-guidance/readability/

### Readability Definition
How easy it is for someone to understand a piece of text

### Who Benefits from Clear Writing
**Clear, structured writing with short sentences helps:**
- People with learning disabilities
- People with dyslexia
- Users on poor internet connections
- Anyone who wants to read once and understand immediately

## 4. HEADINGS & TITLES

### Why Headings Matter
**Headings help people with:**
- **Time pressures:** Scannable content absorbs instantly
- **Stress:** Noticeable headings help find things
- **Multi-tasking:** Clear language when attention is divided
- **Cognitive impairments:** Clear headings = less cognitive load
- **Motor impairments:** Specific headings = less scrolling
- **Visual impairments:** Labeled headings enable screen reader navigation

### Heading Guidelines
**Use clear, concise, front-loaded headings**

✅ **DO:**
- Use specific, meaningful headings
- Front-load headings with a verb
- Structure your page with headings
- Use sentence case for headings and subheadings
- Use statements (not questions)
- Properly label your headings in the CMS

**Use sentence case for:**
- Headings
- Titles
- Subheadings
- Labels

**DO NOT:**
- Capitalize whole words or phrases (hard to read)
- Put full stops in headings/subheadings

**Quote:** "Text presented in sentence case is the most familiar style for most people and, therefore, usually the fastest to read." (Winters 2017, p. 39)

## 5. LINKS

### Avoid "Link Spatter"
**Link spatter = links in middle of sentences/paragraphs**
- Distracts readers
- Looks like chaotic jumble
- Old-fashioned positioning

### Link Placement
**Wherever possible:** Place links below the sentence or list they refer to
**Why:** Separating links from text makes them easier to select on mobile devices

### Link Guidelines
✅ **DO:**
- Make link text meaningful (should stand alone)
- Avoid mid-sentence links
- Front-load your link text
- Make call-to-action (CTA) links specific
- Start CTA links/button text with a verb
- Make CTA links/button text 2-4 words
- Match the destination content
- Use sentence case

❌ **DON'T:**
- Put a full stop after link text

**Resource:** Design London Readability Guidelines (comprehensive link guidance)

## 6. FORMATTING & EMPHASIS

### Capital Letters
**Use sentence case** (capitalize first word only, plus proper nouns)

**Sentence case is easier to read** - most familiar style, fastest to read

**DO NOT capitalize:**
- Whole words or phrases (very hard to read)

### Bold
**Avoid using bold in web content**
If you use too much bold, users struggle knowing where to pay attention

**Rare exceptions acceptable for:**
- Critical safety information
- Legal requirements
- Essential warnings

**Rule:** If considering bold, ask "Is this truly critical information that could cause harm if missed?" If no, don't use bold.

### Italics
**Minimize use** - creates visual noise and confusion
**Disadvantages:** Users with dyslexia

**Only italicize:**
- Names of boats, ships, books, movies, newspapers, plays, films, journals, publications

**DO NOT italicize:**
- Latin phrases (use plain text for common terms like in vivo, in vitro; use quotation marks for less known terms if clarity needed)

### Underline
**NEVER use underline for emphasis**
Underline indicates hyperlinks only

### Exclamation Marks
**Do not use exclamation marks**

### Full Stops
**DO NOT put full stops in:**
- Page names
- Photo captions
- Headings
- Subheadings
- Initials
- Link text

## 7. HTML & ACCESSIBILITY

### HTML Has Function, Not Just Appearance
**Critical principle:** Don't choose formatting tools purely because they look good

**Every formatting tool changes HTML**, which affects:
- Screen readers
- Accessibility tools
- Page structure

### Headings
**Headings have hierarchy:** H1, H2, H3, etc.
- Used to HEAD a new section
- NOT to make text look bigger/better
- Screen readers use heading structure to navigate
- Must use proper hierarchy (H1 → H2 → H3, etc.)

### Quote/Notice Boxes
**Use for actual quotes and notices**
NOT to provide colored background to text
**Why:** Misleads/confuses those relying on HTML to understand page function

### Role Division
**Web designers:** Make stuff "look cool"
**Web editors:** Simply add content with correct function

## 8. JARGON, ACRONYMS & ABBREVIATIONS

### Two Key Questions for Jargon
Before using jargon, ask:
1. **Do my readers know this term and concept?**
2. **How important is it that I use this particular term?**

### Acronyms & Abbreviations
**Treat like jargon** - not everyone knows what they mean

**Guidelines:**
- Make it clear to the reader
- Try to replace with common term/phrase
- Example: Use "right away" instead of "ASAP"
- Use capital letters for acronyms/abbreviations

### First Use Rule
**Always put acronym in brackets after first use of full term**

**Examples:**
- ✅ DO: The Pacific Opportunities Programme at Otago (POPO) Student Team oversees student support within the Division of Health Sciences
- ❌ DON'T: The POPO Student Team oversees student support within the Division of Health Sciences

### Abbreviation Tags
**Very important for screen readers**
- Use abbreviation tags with title attribute
- Hovering shows full name
- Acceptable to use `<abbr>` tag without title IF explained in full on first instance

**Example:** The Faculty of Biomedical Sciences (BMS) is located in Dunedin. BMS offers...

## 9. ACCESSIBLE & INCLUSIVE LANGUAGE

### What is Accessible Language?
**Language that includes everyone**
Ensures language doesn't exclude based on:
- Gender
- Disability
- Cultural background
- Age

**Reference:** Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1

### Why It Matters
**People feel excluded when:**
- They don't understand words or phrases
- Language poses challenges to assistive technologies (text-to-speech)
- Language is not inclusive (gendered assumptions, stereotypes, culturally appropriative phrases)

### Avoid Informal/Colloquial Language
**Not all users understand colloquialisms**

**Examples:**
- ❌ DON'T: Coming to Otago? Don't forget to pack your togs and jandals!
- ✅ DO: It gets very warm in Dunedin so bring swimwear and sandals

- ❌ DON'T: What are you waiting for? Come in and have a yarn
- ✅ DO: We encourage students to come into the Centre and talk to us

### Language Changes on Page
**NZ Government Web Accessibility Standard requirement**

**Success Criterion 3.1.2:** Language of Parts
- Indicate if text changes to another language
- Add `lang` attribute with correct language code
- Common approach: Add to `<p>`, `<span>`, or heading tag

**Example HTML:**
```html
<p>The Māori greeting is <span lang="mi">Kia ora</span>.</p>
```

## 11. OTAGO-SPECIFIC WEB CONVENTIONS

### Official Names
**Always use official names for:**
- University of Otago
- Otago campuses
- Other significant locations
- Correct campus abbreviations

**Examples:**
- ❌ DON'T: Otago University
- ✅ DO: The University of Otago

- ❌ DON'T: Anatomy Department
- ✅ DO: The Department of Anatomy

### Academic Titles
**Do not abbreviate academic titles - use full titles**

**Examples:**
- ✅ DO: Associate Professor Jane Downes welcomed the Prime Minister of Samoa to Otago
- ❌ DON'T: Assoc Prof Mark Winders was recently knighted for his contribution to education

## 13. CONTRADICTIONS & ISSUES IDENTIFIED

### ❌ CONTRADICTION: Bold Usage - RESOLVED
**Print Guide:** "Use bold sparingly to emphasise text you wish to stand out"
**Web Guide:** "Avoid using bold. If you use too much bold users struggle..."

**RESOLUTION:**
- **Web content:** Avoid bold. Rare exceptions only for critical safety/legal information.
- **Print content:** Use bold sparingly for dates, names, deadlines
- **Reason:** Web readability is more critical; too much bold causes scanning issues. The different mediums warrant different approaches.

### ✅ RESOLVED: Latin Phrases in Web Content
**Web content rule:** Do NOT italicize Latin phrases
- Common terms (in vivo, in vitro, in utero): Use plain text
- Less known terms: Use quotation marks if needed for clarity

**Print content rule:** Italicize Latin phrases (from print style guide)

**Why different:** Web italics create visual noise and disadvantage users with dyslexia

### ⚠️ POTENTIAL ISSUE: Capitalization
**Web Guide:** "Use sentence case for headings"
**Print Guide:** "Use capital letter for first word only in headings, unless proper noun"

**NOTE:** These are actually the same rule, just worded differently. No conflict.

## 15. MOST USEFUL ELEMENTS FOR AI TOOLS

### High-Value Rules for AI Implementation

**1. Content Length:**
- Web = 50% of print equivalent
- Users read only 18% of content
- Keep sentences to 15-20 words

**2. Structure Pattern:**
- Inverted pyramid (most important first)
- One topic per paragraph
- Lists over paragraphs
- Front-load key information

**3. Voice & Style:**
- Active voice (not passive)
- Familiar words (not jargon)
- Lean phrases (omit unnecessary words)
- Strong verbs (not hidden noun forms)

**4. Formatting Rules:**
- Sentence case for ALL headings/titles
- Avoid bold (rare exceptions: critical safety/legal info only)
- No exclamation marks (web-specific)
- No underline (reserved for links)
- Minimal italics (publications only, NOT Latin phrases)

**5. Links:**
- Place below text (not mid-sentence)
- Descriptive link text (can stand alone)
- 2-4 words for CTA buttons
- Start with verb
- No full stop after link text

**6. Accessibility Requirements:**
- Plain language (legal requirement for public sector)
- Avoid colloquialisms
- Indicate language changes with `lang` attribute
- Proper heading hierarchy (H1→H2→H3)
- Meaningful link text for screen readers

**7. Otago-Specific:**
- University of Otago (never "Otago University")
- Department of [Name] (never "[Name] Department")
- Full academic titles (never "Assoc Prof")
